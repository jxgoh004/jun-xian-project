The groundwork, done before you start

A lot of investment research is tedious and repeatable. The tools handle that part and leave the judgment call to you.

  1. Fetch the data

    Financials come from Yahoo Finance and valuation metrics from FinViz. Scheduled jobs refresh the screener and the scanner every weekday after the US close.

  2. Run the model

    A 20-year, multi-phase DCF picks its own cash-flow base and a beta-driven discount rate. A vision model grades each chart pattern for quality.

  3. Make the call

    Every auto-filled input is shown with its source and stays editable. The result is a starting point for your own judgment, not a signal.

Questions worth asking first

What the numbers mean, where they come from, and how far to trust them.

Ask me on LinkedIn →
Is this investment advice?

No. Every figure is the output of a model with stated assumptions. Treat it as a starting point for your own research, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Where does the data come from?

Financial statements come from Yahoo Finance. Valuation metrics, beta and growth estimates come from FinViz. Both are free sources, so figures can be delayed or occasionally wrong.

How is intrinsic value calculated?

With a 20-year, multi-phase discounted cash flow model. Years 1–5 use the primary growth rate, years 6–10 use half of it, and years 11–20 use a fixed 4%.

The cash flows are discounted at a rate derived from the stock’s beta, adjusted for net debt, and divided by shares outstanding.

How often is everything updated?

The screener and the pattern scanner are rebuilt by scheduled jobs every weekday after the US market closes. The calculator fetches live data each time you look up a ticker.

Why do some valuations look extreme?

A DCF is very sensitive to its inputs, and one unusual year of cash flow or an optimistic growth estimate can swing the result a long way. Open the stock in the calculator to see every input and adjust the ones you disagree with.

What is an inside bar spring?

A bullish reversal pattern. After a wide mother bar, price coils into a narrow inside bar, briefly breaks below the mother low, and is recovered by the next bar.

The backtested edge is modest. The scanner lists setup candidates, not signals.
